Cold Outreach — Australian Christian Influencers

A practical, operationally-ready strategy for reaching Australian Christian creators, podcasters, pastors, youth leaders, and ministries. Not aspirational — the numbers and conversion rates are realistic.

Author's note on honestyCold outreach to creators is hard. Industry benchmarks for B2B cold email sit around 1–5% reply rate; for cold DM to influencers the realistic reply rate is 0.5–3%, and the conversion from reply to "yes, I'll actually do something" is much lower again. Build the plan so that even if only 1 in 50 prospects engages, the campaign still pays for itself. Everything below assumes that posture.

1. Who to target (with real Australian names, not archetypes)

The Australian Christian creator market is small and tightly clustered. There are roughly 4 tiers:

Tier 1 — Macro Christian creators (100K+ subscribers/followers)

Hard to reach, expensive, but high leverage if they bite. They get pitched constantly.

  • Bryce Crawford (Bryce Crawford Podcast) — @BryceCrawfordPodcast on YouTube, 1.03M subs, 934 videos. AU-based, US-grown street-preaching style, huge US+AU audience. Audience skews 18–35 Christian. Best approach: warm intro through a US-based Christian creator network, or pitch via management email. Cost: $3K–$15K+ per integrated placement, often not for sale.
  • Jamie Zhu (More Jamie Zhu / Jamie Zhu main) — main @jamiezhu ~895K subs, "More Jamie Zhu" channel 19.1K. Sydney-based, street evangelism, Christian apologist. Excellent fit (AU, 20–35 demo, English + Chinese speaking). Manageable via direct email or DM, but he's flooded with pitches. Cost: $1K–$5K+ for a sponsored segment.

Tier 2 — Mid-tier pastors, podcasters, creators (10K–100K)

The sweet spot for cold outreach. They reply more often, are hungry for content, and their audiences are tight-knit.

  • Dominic Steele — The Pastor's Heart — @ThePastorsHeart, 14.3K YT subs, 669 videos, thepastorsheart.net. Long-form interviews with Australian evangelical pastors, leadership, mission focus. Strong fit: his audience IS Christian youth leaders. Cold email via contact form on thepastorsheart.net will reach his producer/assistant.
  • Sheridan Voysey — Writer, broadcaster, founder of Friendship Lab (sheridanvoysey.com), Perth-based. YT 297 subs but has a large email list and ABC/media profile. Good for podcast guest pitch; less for paid placement.
  • Rebecca Lamb Weiss — Australian Christian podcaster, content creator. ~49K views per episode. Cold DM/email on Instagram @rebeccalambweiss reachable.
  • Martin & Millicent Sedra — Echo Church Sydney — Verified AU pastor couple, 49K+ views per interview. Approachable via church contact form.
  • YCFC Podcast — Young Conservatives for Christ — AU conservative Christian podcast, smaller audience but tight niche.
  • Sam Chan, Dave Jensen, Sam Mahdavi, Pre Shunmugam — these are the names that show up repeatedly on AU evangelical podcasts; their networks are the path in.

Tier 3 — Micro creators and youth pastors (1K–10K)

Highest reply rates. They will actually respond to a personal email. Often better ROI than Tier 1 because they say yes.

  • JP — The Adelaide Christian — @TheAdelaideChristian on YT, 2.33K subs, street-interview/evangelism format. Small but highly engaged AU Christian audience. Likely reply.
  • Louie Peasley — Australian Christian podcaster (Wrestling with God). Smaller channel, direct contact possible.
  • Adam Carpenter — Australian youth pastor vlogger, "Why am I a Youth Pastor??" content. Direct personal brand, easy to pitch.
  • Alex Hayes / Christian Faith Podcast — "Taking Mountains" — Small Australian Christian podcast. ~455 views per episode.
  • Sharon (Stories I'd Tell You at Dinner) — Australian Christian podcast host, reachable on Instagram.
  • Edward Schuller (YCFC), Tenielle Neda (After Dinner Mint) — Smaller creators, podcast interview-friendly.
  • The Worlds of Crissa, Faithful + Free, Love Unconditionally AU — Australian Christian Instagram micro-creators (search #christiangirl, #australianchristian, #prayerjourney). Often 500–5K followers, very responsive, will share free product in exchange.

Tier 4 — Institutional channels (often forgotten, often highest ROI)

These are not "influencers" in the creator sense, but they have audiences and authority.

  • Vision Christian Media (vision.org.au) — Australia's Christian broadcaster. Reaches an older demographic but commands trust; pitching them gets on radio/podcast quickly.
  • The Pastor's Heart / thepastorsheart.net — already in Tier 2, but also a gateway to 50+ pastors who appear as guests.
  • Alpha Australia (alpha.org.au) — Major Christian content/discussion program. Free promotion through their network is possible if the resource fits.
  • Hillsong Worship / Planetshakers / Elevation Church (AU) — Mega-church worship channels with millions of subs. Worth one institutional pitch only — they won't do a "sponsored video" but might feature the app in a curriculum recommendation.
  • Australian Christian Channel, Eternity News (eternitynews.com.au), The Melbourne Anglican, Southern Cross (Catholic) — News/media outlets that review faith-based resources.
  • SU Australia (Scripture Union), Youth for Christ Australia, AFES, CMS, Bush Church Aid, Word of Life Australia — Youth-formation orgs. These are your single highest-conversion channel — they buy licences in bulk and evangelise your product to their network.

Demographic filter — the onetoeight sweet spot

Target creators where the audience is 16–25, English-speaking, in Australia, and the creator's content touches at least one of the eight essentials:

  • Silence / stillness / prayer → contemplative prayer accounts, monastic-adjacent creators, Breath Prayer content.
  • Body / physical discipline → Christian fitness creators (AU has a small but real cohort), discipline/lock-in routines.
  • Skills / formation / competence → homesteading Christian creators, Bible study leaders, theology teachers.
  • Digital self-defence / screen sobriety → this is onetoeight's most distinctive lane — search for AU creators talking about phone addiction, screen time, Jonathan Haidt content, Anxious Generation response. Very few exist; you can become the resource they cite.
  • Community / belonging → youth pastors, small group leaders, church planters (Sedra, Jensen, Mahdavi, Shunmugam network).
  • Knowledge without Wi-Fi → homeschool / classical ed / Charlotte Mason AU Christian creators.
  • Nature / creation → bush Christian creators, hiking prayer accounts.
  • Convictions / worldview → apologetics, "worldview summit," catechesis.

2. How to find them

Platform-by-platform search methodology

YouTube — the richest pool. Search terms that work:

  • "Australian Christian" "podcast" — Tier 2/3 pastors and creators
  • "Sydney" OR "Melbourne" OR "Brisbane" "Christian creator"
  • "Australian Christian" "young adults" and "Australian Christian" "youth"
  • "Australian" "Christian" "interview" "podcast"
  • "youth pastor" "Australia" "podcast"
  • "Christian" "apprenticeship" OR "discipleship" "Australia" (formation language)
  • "Christian" "screen time" OR "phone addiction" "Australia" (on-the-nose but uncrowded)
  • Filter by "This week" / "This month" to find active creators you can engage now.

Instagram — highest reply rate, hardest to discover.

  • Hashtags: #australianchristian #christianaustralia #christiancreator #christiangirl #prayerjourney #biblestudyau #sydneychristian #melbournechristian #brisbaneyouth #youthpastorau #christianpodcastaustralia #christiantokau

  • Reels over posts — the creators investing in Reels are the ones open to collabs.
  • Use IG "Suggested for you" on creator profiles you find to discover the network.

TikTok — the 16–25 channel.

  • #christiantok (global, but tag every AU-specific video)
  • #auschristiantok (small, will pop AU creators)
  • #christianau #biblestudyaus #prayerau
  • Search "Christian youth Australia," "Sydney Christian," "Melbourne Christian"
  • Filter for creators posting 3+/week — engagement rate beats follower count at this layer.

Podcasts (Apple Podcasts / Spotify).

  • Search "Australian Christian podcast" in Spotify — list ~30 shows.
  • Cross-reference with YouTube: many pastors upload sermon audio as video podcasts.
  • The Australian Podcast Ranker (tritonranker.com / Spotify Charts) gives monthly AU top charts — check "Religion & Spirituality" category.

Substack / newsletters (skipped by most outreach).

  • Christian creators in AU with Substacks: search substack.com/search/christian, then filter by AU.
  • Sheridan Voysey, et al. — these people are reachable on email directly.

Facebook (yes, still alive for this demographic).

  • AU Christian Facebook Groups: "Australian Christian Creators," "Christian Podcasters Australia," "Australian Youth Ministers Network," "Hillsong Creative" alumni.
  • Posting in these groups converts. A pinned post offering free Mastery Kits to AU youth leaders gets 50+ signups in a week if framed right.

3. Outreach channels and sequences

Ranked by reply rate, not by ease:

  1. Warm introduction — through a mutual connection. 15–40% reply. Use this for Tier 1/2.
  2. Cold email (warm list) — people you know exist, contact info verified. 2–5% reply.
  3. Cold DM (Instagram/TikTok) — micro creators, reply if the message is clearly personal. 0.5–3% reply.
  4. Facebook group post — lowest friction, highest volume, but lowest personalisation. 2–8% engagement.
  5. Cold email (cold list, scraped) — least effective, lowest reply. 0.5–1.5% reply.
  6. Conference booth / in-person — high friction but high conversion when face-to-face happens.

The 3-touch sequence (use this for everyone)

  • Touch 1 (Day 0): Initial outreach. Short, specific, value-first. No ask.
  • Touch 2 (Day 5–7): Value-add. Send a piece of content, a useful link, a relevant introduction — no ask.
  • Touch 3 (Day 12–14): Breakup. "Last note — if not now, totally fine. Closing the loop." Short, warm.

If no reply after Touch 3, mark dead. Re-engage in 90 days with something new.

4. Messaging templates

Copy and adapt. Every pitch must reference something specific about the creator — without that, reply rate drops below 1%.

Cold DM (Instagram/YouTube/Threads) — under 80 words

Hi [name] — love what you're doing on [specific thing they did recently]. I run a free education program for 16–25 year olds on the 8 essentials (silence, body, skills, digital, community, knowledge, nature, convictions). 100% local, no cost, runs on any phone. Would you want a copy of the guide + a code to share with your community? No obligation to post — just wanted it in your hands.

Cold email (preferred for Tier 2/4)

Subject: A free youth program I'd like in your hands

Hi [name],

I'm Brendan, founder of onetoeight — an education and awareness program for 16–25 year olds in Australia. Eight insights (silence, body, skills, digital self-defence, community, knowledge, nature, convictions), not a sales pitch. Fully local, no account, no cost.

Three reasons I'm writing to you:
1. Your work on [specific recent thing they did] is exactly the kind of thing young Australians need alongside ours.
2. The program is complete — guide, app, AI assistant, audio, prayers — and I think you'd genuinely find it useful.
3. I have no expectations. If you look at it and hate it, that's data.

Can I send you a copy of the guide + a code to give your community?

— Brendan

Follow-up (5–7 days later, short)

Hi [name] — bumping this. No pressure, just want to make sure the offer didn't get lost in your inbox. Either way, thanks for what you do for young people in Aus. — B

Breakup email (12–14 days after initial)

Hi [name] — last note from me. If now isn't the right time, totally fine. If you ever want a copy of the onetoeight guide, just reply "send it" any time in the next year. Genuinely no ask. — Brendan

Loom video DM (for Tier 1/2 — highest reply rate)

Record a 60-second personalised video: "Hi [name], I made this just for you — here's why I think onetoeight would resonate with what you're doing on [their channel topic]..." Loom pitches convert at 2–3× the rate of text DMs.

5. The offer

Different tiers, different offers:

  1. Free product (always) — guide PDF + app access + audio. Cost to you: $0. Use for everyone.
  2. Free bulk licence to youth ministry network — $299/yr value. Use for Tier 4 institutions with 30+ leaders.
  3. Free pod setup for their community — onetoeight runs a free 8-week pod on behalf of their audience. Cost: your time. Use for Tier 2 with engaged communities.
  4. Affiliate code (10–20% commission) — once you monetise. Use for sellers (mum bloggers, course creators).
  5. Paid post — $200–$500 for micro, $1K–$5K for mid. Never pay before they've used the product.
  6. Co-creation — guest on their podcast, they guest on yours. Use for thought leaders.
  7. Speaking at their event — free. Use for youth ministry networks, conferences.

First rule: never pay before they've used the product. If they haven't read the guide, they can't authentically share it.

6. Costs and budget tiers

Tier A — Bootstrap ($0–$500, 3 months)

  • Outreach only. Manual DMs, emails, Facebook group posts.
  • Tools: free tier of Hunter.io, Modash trial, manual IG/TikTok research.
  • Expected output: 1–3 Tier 2 collabs, 5–15 Tier 3 features, 1–5 Tier 4 institutional conversations, ~50–200 free Mastery Kit downloads.

Tier B — Lean ($500–$2,500, 3 months)

  • 1 paid micro-influencer feature ($200–$800 on a Tier 3 creator with 2K–10K followers).
  • 1 sponsored podcast appearance ($100–$500).
  • 1 paid Instagram/FB ad test ($200–$500) to a Christian 16–25 audience (AU-targeted).
  • Free Mastery Kit seeding to 50–100 micro creators.
  • Tooling: Modash paid plan, Hunter.io paid, Calendly.
  • Expected output: 5–10 collabs, 10–25 features, 3–8 institutional deals, ~500–2,000 free downloads.

Tier C — Growth ($2,500–$10,000, 3 months)

  • 1 paid Tier 2 creator campaign ($1K–$3K for a dedicated segment).
  • 2–3 paid Tier 3 creators ($500–$1.5K each).
  • 1 institutional sponsorship deal (e.g., pay to be a featured resource in a Christian youth ministry newsletter, $500–$1,500).
  • 1 conference booth or speaking slot ($500–$2,000 — e.g., at SU Australia, Youth Alive, or a regional youth ministry conference).
  • $500–$1,500 in paid ads (Meta + TikTok, AU Christian 16–25 audience).
  • Free Mastery Kit seeding to 100–300 creators.
  • Expected output: 15–30 collabs, 30–75 features, 10–25 institutional conversations, 2–5 closed licence deals, ~2,000–10,000 free downloads.

Tier D — Scale ($10K–$50K/quarter)

  • 2–3 Tier 1 macro creator campaigns ($5K–$15K each).
  • Ongoing paid Tier 2/3 program.
  • Full-time outreach hire or agency.
  • PR push (e.g., Eternity News, The Melbourne Anglican).
  • Conference circuit (3–5 events/year).
  • Expect this to be Year 2 territory, not launch.

7. Australian specifics — what actually matters

Cultural / market context

  • Australian Christianity is post-Christendom, not pre-Christendom. Most young Aussies are "spiritual but not religious" or "nones." Christian creators operate from a minority-but-resilient base; the audience is committed, not casual.
  • Theology skew: Australian evangelical Christianity is broadly Reformed/Anglican/Baptist in the southeast; Catholic charismatic in Sydney; Pentecostal in Brisbane/Gold Coast; smaller evangelical pockets in Adelaide/Perth. The formation kit needs to work across all of these without getting tribal — keep theology at the framework level (8 essentials as universal formation), not the brand level.
  • Generosity culture: AU Christian creators respond well to "we built this for free" / "100% free for users" framing. Lead with the no-cost, no-strings angle. Money-first pitches are immediately suspect in this market.
  • Time zones matter: AEST/AEDT is +10/+11 UTC. Best outreach times: Tues/Wed/Thurs, 9–11am AEST or 7–9pm AEST. Monday is full of catch-up; Friday they sign off early. Don't email on weekends.
  • School holidays affect youth ministry reach. Late March / late June / mid September / early December — youth pastors are off-site at camps. Plan around this.

For onetoeight specifically, expect:

  • Tier 1 outreach: 0.5–2% reply, 0.1–0.5% conversion. Plan for 50 pitches → 1 conversion.
  • Tier 2 outreach: 2–5% reply, 1–3% conversion. Plan for 30–50 pitches → 1 conversion.
  • Tier 3 outreach: 5–15% reply, 3–10% conversion. Plan for 10–20 pitches → 1 conversion.
  • Tier 4 outreach: 10–25% reply, 5–15% conversion. Plan for 7–10 pitches → 1 conversion.

Volume math — what does success look like?

If you send 200 personalised outreach messages over 8 weeks:

  • Expect 8–25 replies (4–12% blended).
  • Expect 2–6 conversations that lead somewhere.
  • Expect 1–3 actual content collaborations or institutional deals.

That is the realistic, achievable outcome. If you get more than that, you've found a channel that works — scale it. If you get less, revisit the offer and the personalisation depth.

When to send

  • Best months for outreach: February, March, August, September (post-holiday, pre-Easter and pre-school-year, when creators are planning content calendars).
  • Worst months: December (creators off), January (recovery), and the week of any major Christian conference (creators are heads-down).
  • Day of week: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday. Send between 9–11am AEST or 7–9pm AEST.
  • Avoid: Friday afternoon, weekends, AU public holidays, school holidays (state-dependent), the week of Easter.

When to give up

  • If no reply to a sequence of 3 touches (initial + value-add + breakup), mark as dead and move on. Do NOT keep emailing — it burns reputation.
  • If a creator replies saying "not now" or "send me the link," follow up in 90 days, not 30.
  • If a creator says "not for me, but have you tried [X]?" — that X is your next best prospect.

How long before you see results

  • First reply: Day 1–3 of campaign.
  • First meeting: Day 7–14.
  • First piece of content live: Day 21–45 (creator needs time to make it).
  • First institutional deal closed: Day 30–90.
  • Real momentum (the inbound requests start): Day 60–120 if you're consistent.
  • "The campaign worked" verdict: 3–6 months. Don't judge it earlier.

9. Tooling stack (recommended)

  • Prospecting: Modash or Heepsy (influencer discovery), Hunter.io (email finding), SparkToro (audience overlap).
  • Outreach: Instantly.ai or Lemlist (cold email at scale), or just Gmail if you're under 100 contacts/week.
  • CRM: Notion or Airtable (free). Track: name, handle, contact info, channel, status, last touch, next action, notes.
  • Scheduling: Calendly (free tier).
  • Tracking: UTM parameters on every link, Bitly for short URLs, linktr.ee for creator aggregation.
  • Content: Canva for one-pagers, Loom for personalised video DMs (raises reply rate by 2–3×).
  • Loom videos: Standout tactic — record a 60-second personalised video for each Tier 1/2 pitch ("I made this video just for you"). Reply rates on Loom DM pitches are dramatically higher.

10. The plan — first 90 days

Week 1–2: Build the list.

  • 200 named prospects across the four tiers.
  • Verify contact info for each.
  • Write 4 messaging templates (one per tier).
  • Build the CRM.

Week 3–6: Outreach sprint, Tier 4 + Tier 3 first.

  • Post in 5+ AU Christian Facebook groups offering free Mastery Kits.
  • Send 100 personalised messages (mix of Tier 3 DM + Tier 4 email).
  • Track every reply, every meeting, every conversion in the CRM.

Week 7–10: Tier 2 outreach + first content goes live.

  • Begin Tier 2 email campaigns.
  • First Tier 3 co-created content pieces should be published now — amplify them.
  • Reach out to AU Christian media (Eternity News) with a press release about the launch.

Week 11–13: Tier 1 outreach + institutional deals.

  • Reach Tier 1 (only via warm intros if possible; cold Tier 1 rarely works).
  • Close institutional deals — these are the revenue.
  • Re-engage anyone who replied but said "not now" with a 90-day follow-up.

Day 90 review:

  • How many replies? (Target: 8–25)
  • How many content pieces? (Target: 5–15)
  • How many institutional conversations? (Target: 5–15)
  • How many licence deals closed? (Target: 1–5)
  • How many free Mastery Kit downloads? (Target: 200–2,000)

If numbers are below target, the problem is usually one of: (a) targeting wrong tier, (b) message too generic, (c) offer not compelling enough, or (d) volume too low. Diagnose, fix, and run another 90 days.

11. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending the same message to everyone. Each pitch should reference something specific to the creator. Without that, reply rate drops to <1%.
  • Pitching paid placement to micro creators. They don't have ad inventory; they want free product and a story.
  • Pitching free product to macro creators. They get pitched constantly for free; cash or equity-style offers work better.
  • Following up more than 3 times. Burns the relationship; you'll never convert them.
  • Promising things you can't ship. Don't promise "your audience will get X downloads" — you don't control virality.
  • Not personalising the offer per tier. A flat "we'll send you a free kit" pitch to a 14K-sub pastor doesn't resonate the same way it does with a 2K-sub street evangelist. Match the offer to the creator's scale and audience.
  • Ignoring Tier 4 (institutions). This is your highest-conversion, highest-revenue channel. The influencer-tier names are sexier but institutions close deals.
  • Burning the only warm intro you have. If someone gives you a warm intro to a Tier 1 creator, do not waste it on a generic pitch. Get the timing and offer right first.
  • Treating a "no" as a "no forever." Most "no"s are "not right now." Build a re-engagement list and circle back in 90 days with something new.
  • Quitting after 4 weeks. Cold outreach to creators compounds. The first month is noisy; month 3 is when inbound starts.

12. The one-line version (if you only remember one thing)

Build a list of 200 named Australian Christian creators and ministry decision-makers, send 100 personalised multi-touch sequences over 8 weeks offering them something genuinely useful (free product, a guest spot, a co-created piece, or a bulk licence), and plan for a 2–6% reply rate that converts 1–3% to actual action — and treat institutional partnerships (churches, youth ministries, Christian schools) as your highest-leverage channel, not the macro-influencer chase.
