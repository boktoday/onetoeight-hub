The Circles App: eight friends, eight weeks, eight essentials

A group accountability app for teenagers. Form a circle of up to eight friends. Each works through one essential per week together — in the app and in real life. The circle keeps everyone honest, and everyone shares the cost.

"Most formation content is consumed alone. This makes it something you do with people who know your name."

The premise

Social formation, not solo improvement

The eight essentials work best with others. The guide already says it: "Use it with at least one other honest person." This app makes that happen structurally.

A circle = 3 to 8 friends who commit to eight weeks together — one essential per week. The app guides them through each essential, prompts face-to-face meetups, tracks progress, and surfaces accountability nudges.

Each person buys a "seat" in the circle. A full circle of 8 at $10 each = $80 for the 8-week programme. Seats are tied to the specific circle. Friends have skin in the game together.

Experience design

How the app feels to use

🔗

Circle formation

One person creates a circle, sends invite links to up to 7 friends. Each person claims their seat by paying $10.

📅

8-week calendar

Each week unlocks one essential. Monday: intro + audio. Wednesday: group challenge. Friday: check-in. Sunday: face-to-face prompt.

✅

Accountability nudges

If you miss two check-ins in a row, the circle gets notified. Gentle heat, not shame. You agreed to this.

👋

Face-to-face prompts

"This week, sit in silence together for 10 minutes. Then share what came up." or "Go outside with someone from the circle."

🔥

Streak + circle score

Individual streaks for daily practice. Circle score = combined completion rate. A circle with 90%+ completion gets a completion badge.

🤝

Buddy pairs

Each week the circle pairs you with a different person. You check in once mid-week directly: "Did you do the silence practice?"

🎯

Weekly skill together

Essential 3 (Skills) week prompts: "Cook a meal together." Essential 5 (Community): "Show up for someone." Essential 7 (Nature): "Walk outside together without phones."

🛡️

Optional anonymity

Usernames only within the circle. No public profiles, no followers. The circle is a private room, not a performance stage.

IRL prompts by essential

What to do face-to-face each week

Week 1 · Silence

"Sit together in silence for 10 minutes. No phones. Then share one thing that surfaced."

Week 2 · Body

"Go for a walk or do a bodyweight workout together. Talk about one gate you're choosing to guard."

Week 3 · Skills

"Cook a meal together from scratch. Each person brings one ingredient they've never used before."

Week 4 · Digital

"Do a 'phone swap' for 30 minutes. Hold each other's phones. Discuss what you noticed."

Week 5 · Community

"Do something for someone outside the circle together. Meal, visit, errand, note."

Week 6 · Knowledge

"Teach each other one skill you know that the rest don't. 10 minutes each."

Week 7 · Nature

"Go outside together for an hour. No phones. Walk somewhere without a destination."

Week 8 · Convictions

"Share your conviction statements with each other. Everyone keeps everyone else's. Follow up in a month."

Circle economics

How money flows

One person starts the circle and pays $80 to claim all 8 seats, or each person pays $10 individually. If someone drops out after week 1, the seat can be reassigned to someone else (the circle stays whole).

The $80 pays for: 8 weeks of guided essential content, audio for each essential, the accountability system, buddy pairing, weekly face-to-face prompts, completion score tracking, and a certificate at the end.

Psychological design

Why the circle model works

Mechanic 01

Sunken cost + peer commitment

Everyone paid. Everyone knows everyone paid. Missing a check-in means letting the circle down, not just yourself. This is far stronger than individual accountability.

Mechanic 02

Buddy rotation

A new pair each week prevents cliques and spreads relational weight. By week 8, everyone has connected 1:1 with everyone else at least once.

Mechanic 03

IRL > digital

The app is the skeleton. The real work happens in rooms, on walks, in kitchens. The app prompts face-to-face but doesn't try to replace it.

Mechanic 04

Natural viral loop

Each circle is 8 teens. If even half finish, they become advocates. Each one can start or join a new circle next term. Circles multiply.

Risks to solve for

What could go wrong and how

Dropout mid-programme

Seat can be reassigned to a new member. The circle stays at full strength. No refunds after week 2 encourages commitment.

One person dominates

Buddy pairs prevent one voice taking over. The app gives everyone equal airtime through structured prompts.

Nothing happens IRL

If the group never meets, the app prompts for at least one meetup. Circle score is visible. No meetups = lower score.

Friends just game the system

Irish prompts require photo proof (group photo at meetup). Lightweight, not surveillance. But enough to prevent fake completions.

Teen psychology upgrade

What teens actually want — and how the app gives it to them

Most formation programmes fail with teens because they're designed for adults. The circles model works if it's built around what young people already desire, not what adults want them to desire.

🧭 Autonomy

Teens want: "I chose this. It's not imposed on me."
The app gives: No adult supervision in the pod. No youth pastor dashboard. The pod chooses its own name, culture, and rhythm within the 8-week structure. You can skip a week (the pod nudges, not the app).

👥 Belonging

Teens want: "I have my people. They see me."
The app gives: A small, private group that shares an experience over time, not broadcast. By week 8, these 8 people know each other differently. Buddy rotation ensures everyone connects 1:1.

⚡ Competence

Teens want: "I'm good at something real."
The app gives: Concrete skills by week 3 (cook, fix, budget). A visible completion score. A certificate at the end that says "I finished something hard with people I trust."

🎮 Play

Teens want: "This doesn't feel like homework."
The app gives: Pod names, circle scores, buddy mystery, silent partners, photo proof missions. But the stakes are real enough that it's not disposable.

🔒 Privacy

Teens want: "Adults aren't watching this."
The app gives: No parent portal. No pastor report. The pod exists in its own walled garden. The only external view is the completion certificate, which they control who sees.

👁️ Realness

Teens want: "This isn't fake church energy."
The app gives: No cheesy graphics, no hyped language, no guilt. The same tone as the guide: direct, honest, adult. The app trusts them to take it seriously because they chose it.

Product design upgrades

Small tweaks that change everything

Rename

Pod, not Circle

"Circle" feels like church small group energy. "Pod" feels intimate, self-contained, slightly mysterious. Teens name their own pod. The app never assigns a default.

Friction removal

Starter kit for the first meetup

The app generates a 1-page PDF: "First Pod Meeting Agenda." Introductions > Pick a pod name > Read the Essential 01 scenario > Do 3 minutes of silence > Choose your weekly check-in time. The hardest step is the first one.

Anticipation

Silent partners

Each week, one person is assigned as your silent partner. They watch your progress but don't interact. At the end of 8 weeks, all silent partners reveal themselves and share one observation. Zero extra work, massive emotional payoff.

Off-ramp

Bounded commitment

8 weeks, then it's done. Not forever. Teens fear indefinite commitment. A clear end date makes starting feel possible. After 8 weeks, the pod can renew, switch members, or disband with honour.

Flexibility

Solo mode exists

Not everyone has 7 friends ready to commit. Solo mode: same content, no accountability, $5 for 8 weeks. Option to join a partial pod with matching. This solves the chicken-and-egg problem and gives non-johners a path in.

Real reward

Completion is something real

Not a digital badge. A printable "Letter of Completion" with each pod member's name. A discount code for the next pod. Access to an alumni group where past pods share what they did. Something they can show someone.

"Eight weeks. Eight people. One essential per week. No adults. No fake energy. Just a group of people who decided to build roots together instead of scrolling alone."

Positioning

What this competes with (and what it isn't)

It's not a Bible study app

Those feel like homework. This feels like a challenge you opted into with friends.

It's not a self-improvement course

Those are solo and dry. This is social and physical. You meet in rooms, not just in comments.

It's not a social media detox app

Those tell you to put your phone down alone. This gives you people to put it down with.

It's not a youth group programme

Those are led by adults. This is peer-to-peer. The youth pastor doesn't get a seat.

Technical

What would need building

Web app (first)

Circle creation, invite links, payment, weekly content delivery, check-ins, scoreboard, buddy assignments. Could ship as PWA with no app store needed.

Mobile app (later)

Push notifications for check-ins, nudge reminders, photo upload for IRL proof, audio player for essentials. Native feels better for teen adoption.

Smart MVP path: Ship as a mobile-optimised web app first using the existing content. Sign up → form circle → pay → get week 1 content → check-in system. If it works at all, the rest follows. Build in public with the first 50 circles as beta testers.

"The eight essentials were always meant to be done with other people. The circles app just makes that the default instead of an afterthought."

The big picture

What this unlocks

Right now, onetoeight is a guide. A good one, but a solo experience most people buy and never finish.

The circles model turns it into a social programme with natural accountability and a built-in distribution engine. Each circle that completes is 8 more people who:

  • experienced the full eight essentials
  • have a shared language to talk about it
  • are likely to invite friends into the next circle
  • become free marketing through real-world proof

It also solves the biggest problem with Christian curriculum: people buy it but don't run it. Circles makes them run it because the group expects it.

Next steps

How to test this without building anything

  1. Ask 2–3 real teen friend groups: "Would you pay $10 each to do an 8-week challenge together with an app?"
  2. Run one circle manually: Use a WhatsApp group + the PDF guide. Send weekly prompts manually. See if they stay engaged.
  3. If yes: Build the web app MVP. Start with 10 beta circles (80 teens).
  4. If no: Learn exactly why and adjust the model before building anything.
