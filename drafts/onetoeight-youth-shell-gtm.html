Youth Phone Shell + Go-To-Market Strategy

How onetoeight presents to young people, how it reaches the adults who buy for them, and how it earns without becoming another wellness app.

This document covers the phone-first design system, distribution channels, pricing architecture, launch sequence, content marketing, partnerships, and the metrics that actually matter. It is written for Brendan O'Keefe and the onetoeight team. It assumes the product is formation, not entertainment. The tone is direct because the audience is tired of hype.

Section 1

Youth Phone Shell Design System

The phone shell is the surface young people actually touch. It is not the leader guide. It is not the pitch deck. It is the thing a 15-year-old opens at 10pm when they cannot sleep. The design must feel like a tool, not a tract.

Design principle: Warm, earthy, serious. Cream field, terracotta action, sage support, umber structure. Fraunces for voice, Inter for utility. No neon, no glassmorphism, no confetti. Progress is marked, not celebrated.

1.1 Core Interaction Model

The shell is built around cards, not chapters. Each essential is a stack of eight cards:

CardPurposeContent
00 CoverOrientationEssential number, title, one-line promise, audio play button, time estimate
01 StoryHookShort narrative scene (300–500 words) that makes the essential concrete
02 WhyStakesThree bullets first, full paragraph behind a "go deeper" expander
03 RealityHonestyThe hard part. What usually goes wrong. No sugar-coating.
04 PracticeActionThe weekly challenge, broken into daily steps
05 This WeekCommitmentAction-first challenge ticket: level chip, time estimate, mode (solo/with someone/offline), one CTA
06 TalkDiscussionTwo or three questions that actually work in a group
07 WisdomAnchorSeven day-chips, one verse or reflection per day, tap to expand
08 ChecklistClosureMark done, export fragment, next essential preview

1.2 Sticky HUD

The top of the screen never scrolls away. It shows:

  • Silence · 01/08 · 12 min · continue
  • Four actions: Listen · Do · Discuss · Mark done

This is the app's spine. It tells the young person where they are, how much is left, and what to do next without making them think.

1.3 Action-First Challenges

Challenges are the hero UI object, bigger than reflections. Each challenge is a ticket:

Level 2 Solo 8 min

Cook one meal from scratch

No packet mixes. No takeaway. Plan it, shop for it, cook it, eat it. Take a photo of the mess.

CTA: Start challenge →

Young people skim. The ticket format forces clarity. If a challenge cannot fit on a ticket, it is too complicated.

1.4 Postable Fragments

Every essential generates auto-formatted mini-cards for screenshot sharing:

  • One line of truth from the chapter
  • The essential number and title
  • The onetoeight mark
  • Cream background, terracotta accent, Fraunces type

Young creators distribute culture through captures. If the layout does not make shareable units, it dies in the chat. The share button uses the native Web Share API — WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, iMessage. No in-app social network to moderate.

1.5 Progress Without Cheap Gamification

Formation is not a streak. The shell tracks progress honestly:

What we doWhat we do not do
"2 of 8 essentials started" — calm, factualFlame icons, daily streaks, XP bars
Checkmarks that tick when a challenge is doneConfetti, badges, leaderboards by default
Pod score as a group health signal (optional)Individual ranking against friends
Silent partner — someone watches without commentingPublic shaming or guilt notifications
Service extension unlocked after week 8Pay-to-win or skip-ahead mechanics

The leaderboard is optional in the subscription tier because some youth groups want it. It is off by default. Formation is not a competition.

1.6 Typography & Colour System

Type

  • Fraunces — headings, essential numbers, quotes. Serif with warmth.
  • Inter — body, UI labels, buttons. Clean, legible at small sizes.
  • Mobile body: 17–18px, line-height 1.6
  • Measure: 32–36 characters on phone
  • Essential number display: huge (01, 02, 03…)

Colour by Essential

  • Silence — sage soft
  • Body — terracotta
  • Skills — umber hard
  • Digital Defence — ink edge
  • Community — almond warm
  • Knowledge — terracotta line
  • Nature — sage strong
  • Convictions / Tested Faith — umber + cream high contrast

1.8 Technical Constraints

  • PWA first. Runs in any modern browser. Add to home screen. No app store gatekeeping for the base experience.
  • Android APK via Capacitor. For users who want a native install. Same codebase, same components.
  • Local-first. IndexedDB + localStorage. No account, no cloud, no data leaving the device unless the user exports.
  • WebLLM local AI. Llama 3.2 1B (~780MB) runs in-browser via WebGPU. RAG knowledge base of all 8 essentials. Works offline after first download. No API, no server, no COPPA/GDPR server-side liability.
  • Web Share API. All sharing goes through apps the young person already uses. No in-app chat to build, moderate, or secure.

2.1 Channel Details

Primary

Email to Youth Pastors

The highest-converting channel. Youth pastors are reachable, budget-aware, and desperate for programming that does not feel like babysitting.

  • Build a list from denominational directories, conference attendee lists, and Facebook Group admins
  • Subject lines: "8 weeks, 8 kids, $10 each" / "The silence session made my teen put her phone down"
  • Send a free PDF of Essential 01 (Silence) as the lead magnet
  • Follow-up sequence: 3 emails over 10 days — problem, product, proof
Primary

Facebook Groups for Youth Ministry

There are active communities of youth pastors sharing curriculum, games, and survival tips. These are high-trust, high-intent spaces.

  • Join as a participant, not a vendor. Answer questions. Share the free Silence chapter when relevant.
  • Post the "3 of them now cook Sunday lunch" story as a case study, not an ad
  • Offer a free leader pack to the first 10 groups that run the programme
Primary

Instagram + TikTok (Parent-Facing)

Parents of teens are on Instagram and Facebook. They are worried, tired, and looking for someone to tell them it is not too late.

  • Content: 60-second clips of a parent reading the Silence chapter, a teen cooking for the first time, a before/after screen-time stat
  • Hook: "You gave them a phone. Did you give them roots?"
  • CTA: link in bio to the free chapter, then email capture
Secondary

Podcasts

Christian parenting and youth ministry podcasts are always looking for guests. Brendan has a story: why he built this, what the eight essentials are, what happened when teens tried silence.

  • Target: youth ministry podcasts, Christian parenting shows, education reform shows
  • Offer: free Mastery Kit for the host, discount code for listeners
Secondary

Conferences & Events

Youth ministry conferences (e.g. Youth Specialties, Orange Conference, denominational gatherings) are where curriculum buying happens in person.

  • Booth or table with printed guides, phone demos, and a sign-up sheet
  • Run a 20-minute workshop: "How to run an 8-week formation programme with zero prep"
  • Collect emails, follow up within 48 hours
Secondary

Direct to Schools (Universal Edition)

The Universal Edition is designed for secular settings. Sell to principals, chaplains, PDHPE coordinators, and wellbeing officers.

  • Email principals directly with a one-page PDF: "8 weeks, 8 essentials, no faith requirement"
  • Offer a free staff training webinar for any school that buys a licence
  • Target alternative education programmes, homeschool co-ops, and community centres first — they move faster than districts
Viral

Peer-to-Peer (Young People)

The $10 pod seat is designed for virality. One kid buys, seven friends join. The app makes sharing easy; the challenge makes it worth sharing.

  • Postable fragments are the viral unit — one line of truth, screenshot, send to group chat
  • Pod codes make joining frictionless — no account, no password, just a code
  • The "3-minute podcast" feature gives young people a reason to create and share audio
Section 3

Pricing & Packaging Strategy

The pricing architecture is designed to remove friction at the bottom and capture value at the top. A teenager can start for $10. A school can pay $1,500. Everything in between serves a different buyer with a different budget.

3.1 Product Architecture

ProductPriceBuyerWhat It IncludesRole
Pod Seat$10 / 8 weeksYoung person (or parent)App access, pod membership, weekly challenges, AI assistant, podcast recording, evidence upload, service extension eligibilityViral entry point. Gets young people in. Pays for itself in coffee.
Mastery Kit$44.88Parent, leader, serious individualFull guide (140+ pages), audio narration, 80 prayers/reflections, 56 wisdom references, leader pack, mini course, offline AI tools, app accessCore revenue. The "I want everything" option. Priced below a video game.
Youth Group Licence$299 / yearYouth pastor, church, community org30 user seats, leader pack, mini course, audio, both Christian + Universal editions, discounted scalingInstitutional anchor. Turns one pastor into 30 users.
Youth Subscription$5.99 / monthIndividual teen or parentWeekly challenges, progress tracking, audio offlining, group discussion prompts, optional leaderboardRetention layer. Recurring revenue for ongoing engagement after the 8 weeks.
School / District Licence$1,500Principal, district, wellbeing officerFull institutional licence, both editions, all resources, staff training materials, lifetime updates, tailored adoption supportTop-tier institutional. High touch, high margin, high impact.

3.2 Pricing Logic

  • $10 pod seat: Impulse-buy territory for a teen with a part-time job. Eight friends at $10 each = $80 per pod. That is less than a youth group pizza night. The price signals "this is for you," not "this is for your parents."
  • $44.88 Mastery Kit: Anchored against a video game ($70) or a pair of sneakers ($120). The .88 ending is intentional — it signals precision and accessibility, not luxury. 100 kits = $4,488. 1,000 = $44,880.
  • $299 youth group licence: Most youth ministries have $200–$800/year for curriculum. $299 sits comfortably in that band. It is less than $10/month for 30 seats. The licence includes both editions, which removes the "which one do we buy" objection.
  • $5.99/month subscription: Comparable to Spotify or Netflix. Positioned as "the cost of one coffee per month for a tool that actually helps." The subscription is for after the 8 weeks — retention, not acquisition.
  • $1,500 school licence: Schools spend thousands on textbooks and PD. $1,500 for a full institutional licence with staff training is a rounding error in a school budget. It is high enough to signal seriousness, low enough to avoid procurement hell.

3.3 Bundling & Upsell Paths

The Pod Path

Free chapter → $10 pod seat → finish 8 weeks → $5.99/mo subscription for ongoing challenges → buy Mastery Kit as a gift or reference.

The Leader Path

Free leader pack → $44.88 Mastery Kit to evaluate → $299 youth group licence for the whole group → renew annually → refer another pastor.

The Parent Path

Instagram ad → free Silence chapter → $44.88 Mastery Kit for the family → buy pod seats for the teen and their friends → gift to another parent.

The School Path

Email → free Universal sample → $1,500 school licence → staff training webinar → renewal + expansion to other year levels.

3.4 What Not To Do

  • Do not offer a free tier of the app. Free attracts tourists, not formation. The free chapter is the sample; the pod seat is the commitment.
  • Do not discount the pod seat below $10. It is already impulse-priced. Discounting signals desperation.
  • Do not bundle the subscription with the pod seat. They serve different phases: pod = 8 weeks, subscription = after.
  • Do not hide the Christian edition behind a paywall while giving the Universal away free. Both are paid. The free sample is Essential 01 only.

Section 5

Content Marketing Approach

Content marketing for onetoeight is not about building an audience of young people. It is about building trust with the adults who buy for them, and giving young people fragments worth sharing. The content is proof, not promotion.

5.1 Content Pillars

PillarAudienceFormatFrequencyExample
The ProblemParents, pastorsShort video, carousel post, email2x / week"7 hours a day. That is how long the average teen spends on a screen. Here is what it costs them."
The ProofPastors, parentsCase study, testimonial, photo essay1x / week"Three of them now cook Sunday lunch for the whole church. That is not a metaphor."
The PracticeYoung people, leadersChallenge demo, audio clip, fragment card3x / week"Try 3 minutes of silence today. No phone. No music. Just sit. Here is what happens."
The ProductAllBehind-the-scenes, feature demo, pricing explanation1x / week"Why we built the AI to run on your phone, not our server."

5.2 Channel-Specific Content

Email (Pastors + Parents)

  • Weekly: One essential, one story, one action. Subject line: "Essential 03: Skills — your teen can fix a tap by Friday."
  • Monthly: Roundup of testimonials, new features, and a discount code for Mastery Kits.
  • Automated: 3-email welcome sequence for new signups — problem, product, proof.

Instagram / TikTok (Parents)

  • Reels: 60-second clips of a parent reading the guide, a teen doing a challenge, a before/after stat.
  • Carousels: "8 things your teen needs that school did not teach them" — swipe through, CTA to free chapter.
  • Stories: Polls ("Does your teen know how to budget?"), Q&A boxes, link stickers.

Facebook Groups (Youth Pastors)

  • Participate in discussions. Do not sell. When someone asks "any good curriculum on screen time?", share the free Silence chapter.
  • Post case studies with permission. "A pastor in Queensland ran this. Here is what happened."
  • Offer to do a free Zoom training for any group that buys a licence.

YouTube / Podcast (Long-Form)

  • YouTube: 10-minute videos — "What is formation?" / "Why silence is a superpower" / "How to run a pod."
  • Podcast: Weekly 20-minute episodes — one essential per episode, interview with a young person who completed it, practical tips for leaders.

Postable Fragments (Young People)

  • Auto-generated from the app: one line of truth, essential number, onetoeight mark.
  • Designed for screenshot and share to WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram Stories.
  • No logo watermark. No URL. Just the line and the mark. If it is good, people will ask where it came from.

Section 6

Partnership Strategy

Partnerships are force multipliers. They give onetoeight access to audiences that would take years to build alone. The strategy is to partner with organisations that already have trust with the target audience, not to build parallel infrastructure.

6.1 Partnership Tiers

TierPartner TypeWhat They GetWhat onetoeight GetsExample
Tier 1: DistributionDenominations, dioceses, large church networksCustom edition, bulk pricing, co-branded leader trainingAccess to hundreds of youth groups, institutional credibilityA Baptist Union or Catholic diocese adopts onetoeight as recommended curriculum
Tier 2: ChannelYouth ministry conferences, podcast networks, curriculum marketplacesBooth fee waiver, affiliate commission, exclusive contentDirect access to decision-makers, speaking slots, email list growthYouth Specialties or Orange Conference features onetoeight in a workshop
Tier 3: ContentChristian parenting influencers, education bloggers, family YouTubersFree Mastery Kit, affiliate link (20%), early access to new featuresAuthentic promotion to engaged audiences, social proofA parenting Instagram account with 50k followers runs the programme with their teen and posts about it
Tier 4: CommunityLocal businesses, community centres, librariesPrize draw sponsorship, service extension visibility, local goodwillReal-world anchors for the service extension, local press, foot trafficA local hardware store sponsors the "Fix" challenge prize draw

6.2 Priority Partnerships (First 6 Months)

1. Denominational Youth Departments

Every denomination has a youth ministry office. They recommend curriculum to their churches. One endorsement can mean 50 youth group licences.

Approach: Offer a free Mastery Kit to the youth director, a custom report on outcomes, and a co-branded training webinar.

2. Homeschool Co-ops

Homeschool families are already formation-focused. They buy curriculum directly, move fast, and talk to each other.

Approach: Offer the Universal Edition at a group rate, plus a free leader pack for the co-op coordinator.

3. Christian Schools

Christian schools want formation content that is not just Bible trivia. The Christian Edition fits their chapel and PDHPE programmes.

Approach: Pitch the school licence with staff training included. Emphasise the measurable outcomes: cooking, budgeting, silence.

4. Youth Ministry Conferences

Conferences are where pastors discover new curriculum. A workshop slot is worth more than a booth.

Approach: Submit a workshop proposal: "8 Weeks to Formation: A Phone-First Programme for Screen-Born Teens." Offer a free pod seat to every attendee.

5. Christian Parenting Podcasts

Podcast hosts need guests. Brendan has a story and a product. A single interview can drive hundreds of Mastery Kit sales.

Approach: Pitch the story: "Why I built a formation app that runs entirely on the device, with no cloud, no data, and no surveillance." Offer a discount code for listeners.

6. Local Businesses (Service Extension)

The service extension needs local prize sponsors. Businesses get goodwill and foot traffic. Young people get real-world rewards.

Approach: Ask a local café, hardware store, or bookshop to sponsor a prize draw. Feature them in the app and in social posts.

6.3 Partnership Rules

  • No exclusivity deals in year one. onetoeight needs to learn what works before locking in.
  • No white-labelling. The brand is the trust. Do not let a partner rebrand it.
  • Always keep the free chapter free. No partner gets to gate the sample.
  • Track partner-driven revenue separately. Know which partnerships pay and which are vanity.
Section 7

Metrics That Matter

Do not track vanity metrics. Downloads, page views, and social likes do not pay bills or prove formation. Track the numbers that show whether young people are actually changing, and whether the business is sustainable.

7.1 Formation Metrics (Product)

MetricTargetWhy It Matters
Pod completion rate60% of pods finish all 8 weeksFormation is the product. If pods do not finish, the product is broken.
Weekly challenge completion70% of active users complete the weekly challengeAction is the point. Listening without doing is entertainment.
Buddy check-in rate50% of buddies check in mid-weekAccountability is the mechanism. If buddies do not check in, the pod is a ghost town.
Evidence upload rate40% of users upload evidence at least onceProof of practice. Uploads show the challenge left the screen and entered the real world.
Podcast recording rate30% of users record at least one 3-minute reflectionCreation over consumption. Recording means the user internalised the essential enough to speak about it.
Service extension uptake25% of finishers start the service trackFormation continues beyond the programme. This is the long-term impact signal.

7.4 What Not To Track

  • App downloads without activation. A download is not a user.
  • Social media followers. Followers do not buy; email subscribers do.
  • Time in app. More time is not better. The app should get out of the way so formation can happen in the real world.
  • AI chat message count. The AI is a tool, not a companion. High usage might mean the content is unclear.
  • Daily active users. Formation is weekly, not daily. DAU is a social media metric.
