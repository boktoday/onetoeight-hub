How to market the eight essentials to four audiences at once

A go-to-market strategy for the Christian Edition of onetoeight, covering youth pastors, parents, teachers, and young people themselves — with channel plans, launch sequence, and A/B tests.

The landscape

Why timing matters now

$10BChristian / classical education market (Forbes 2025)
8.7%K-12 private education CAGR (Research & Markets)

The Christian education and formation sector is growing fast. Classical Christian schooling, homeschool co-ops, and youth ministry curriculum spending are all rising. At the same time, parent anxiety about screen time is at an all-time high (Pew, Statista 2025).

Most existing curricula are either purely theological (no practical skills) or purely practical (no faith content). onetoeight's position is unique: both a practical formation programme and explicitly Christian, with a secular twin for school use.

Youth pastors report finding curriculum primarily through: peer recommendations, conferences, YouTube channels, and Facebook groups. Direct email outreach to leaders remains the highest-converting channel.

Channel strategy

Where to show up and how

Channel 01

Instagram (highest priority — reaches parents AND youth)

Format: Reels (15–60 sec) + static carousel posts for deeper concepts

Content pillars:

  • The Diagnosis — stat cards (7 hrs screen time, 1 in 5 anxiety)
  • The Offer — pull quotes from the guide
  • The Proof — testimonial cards from real users
  • The Practice — one practice per post from "Do This This Week" challenges

Tactic: Use trending Reel audio layered under the Ryan TTS voiceover. Call to action: "Link in bio to start."

Channel 02

TikTok (youth direct)

Format: 15–30 sec, raw, unpolished. Young people trust messy more than produced.

Content: "3 things nobody tells you about growing up with a phone" style. Use guide content as scripts.

Voice: Ryan TTS voiceover over simple text-on-screen. Clean, direct, unapologetic.

Hashtags: #christianyouth #screentime #faithjourney #onetoeight

Channel 03

YouTube (youth pastors + young people)

Two series:

  • For leaders: "How to run the eight essentials" — behind the guide walkthroughs, leader tips
  • For youth: Chapter-by-chapter narrated walkthroughs (all audio already exists)

Format: Chapter audio + simple visuals or slides. Low barrier to produce — existing audio files are ready.

Channel 04

Email (highest-converting for curriculum sales)

Two lists: Pastors/leaders + parents who opt in via Instagram/website

Launch sequence (4 emails):

  1. "You already know the problem. Here's a framework."
  2. "What the eight essentials look like in a youth group"
  3. "The $44.88 Kit that covers 8 weeks of programming"
  4. "Price goes up in 72 hours" (limited-time launch urgency)
Channel 05

Christian podcasts (reach leaders at scale)

Target shows: Youth ministry podcasts (Rooted Ministry, Youth Ministry Podcast, Fuller Youth Institute podcast)

Pitch angle: "We made a Christian AND a secular version of the same guide — here's why that matters." Unique hook that most curricula don't have.

Channel 06

Facebook Groups (youth pastor peer networks)

Target groups: "Youth Ministry Network", "Youth Pastor Theologian", "Reformed Youth Ministry", "The Source for Youth Ministry"

Tactic: Not selling. Offering. "I built a free 8-week framework for screen-born teens — here's the intro PDF, happy to share the whole thing."

Product + pricing

How the products fit together

Christian Edition

$44.88 — Full guide + audio + prayers + scripture + leader pack. The primary product.

Universal Edition

$44.88 — Non-religious version. Schools, community groups. Bulk license potential.

Youth Field Edition (free)

Carded phone shell. Distribution & growth engine — leads to full purchase.

Print-on-demand guide

~$18 via Amazon. Low margin, high reach for those who prefer physical books.

Pricing note: $44.88 positions the Kit as accessible but not free. Many curriculum products cost $100–$300. This is intentionally lower to reduce friction for first-time buyers from smaller churches.
