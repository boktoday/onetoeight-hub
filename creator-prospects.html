How to sequence the rest

Three tiers. Work them in order.

Big names are harder to reach but multiply your signal. Mid-tier is the sweet spot — reachable, engaged, and hungry for good content. Small-but-engaged creators often say yes fastest because nobody else is asking them.

Tier 1 — Big reach

The amplifiers

100K+ audience. Harder to get a reply, but one share changes everything. Approach with a specific, low-friction ask — not "partner with us" but "here's a free resource your audience can use this week."

Jamie Zhu (895K YT, 2.4M TikTok) · Fr. Rob Galea (148K IG) · Dave Adamson (189K IG) · Keiara Dorrough (62.6K YT)
Tier 2 — Mid-tier sweet spot

The believers

10K–100K. Reachable, active, and usually making content because they care, not because it pays. These are the ones who'll actually run the program with their own youth group and tell you how it went.

Timothy Yi (37.2K YT) · Dan Paterson (20.7K YT) · The Pastor's Heart (14.3K YT)
Tier 3 — Small but engaged

The early adopters

Under 10K, but tight communities and high trust. They say yes fast, give honest feedback, and become your case studies. PastorSKAR is the standout — a Baptist pastor evangelising through Fortnite, already featured on the ABC.

PastorSKAR (5.32K YT) · Aussie Pastor (2.12K YT) · SU Australia (org, small YT but deep youth network)
How to actually reach them

The contact ladder. Top rung first.

YouTube is the contact goldmine — the "View email address" button in the About tab is the most reliable public business email on the internet. TikTok and Instagram are for discovery and warm-up, not the cold ask.

1

YouTube business email

Check the channel's About tab → "For business inquiries" → "View email address." This is the single highest-yield move. Jamie Zhu's email was right there in his channel description.

Confirmed for: Jamie Zhu (×2), The Pastor's Heart (via church), SU Australia (org email)
2

Link-in-bio → website contact

Linktree, Beacons, or their own site. Usually has an email or a "work with me" form. Dave Adamson's daveadamson.tv and Fr. Rob Galea's frrobgalea.com both have booking/contact paths.

Check for: Keiara Dorrough, Dan Paterson, Timothy Yi, PastorSKAR
3

Agency or management

For anyone over ~50K, the bio often says "MGMT: name@agency." That's actually better than a personal email — it's monitored and expects partnership pitches. Fr. Rob Galea's bookings go through his site.

Likely for: Jamie Zhu (partnerships@ is already a managed address), Fr. Rob Galea
4

Instagram DM — last resort

Lowest reply rate. Only after rungs 1–3 fail. Warm it up first: follow, like a few posts, leave a genuine comment. Then DM. Timothy Yi is DM-only (IG @tim_yi, 891 followers — he'll see it).

Fallback for: Timothy Yi, Dave Adamson, anyone with no public email
The cold email that gets a reply
Subject: Free 8-week program for your [youth group / audience / community]

Hi [name], I've been watching your [channel / content] for a while — especially [specific recent video or post]. You're already doing the hard part: getting Australian teens to think about faith and character in a world that's pulling them the other direction.

We've built a free, local-first eight-week program called onetoeight — eight essentials for a screen-born generation (stillness, nature, digital self-defence, real community, tested faith). No account, no cost, no catch. Kids run it in pods of eight with their own friends. I think your audience would genuinely get something out of it, and I'd love to send you the guide to look over — no strings.

Worth a quick look?

— Brendan
Rules: 3 sentences max in the first paragraph · reference a SPECIFIC piece of their content (proves you're not spamming) · lead with what you're giving, not what you want · one CTA · send Tue–Thu 7–9am AEST · 3-touch follow-up over 2 weeks · never attach a PDF on first contact — link to the hub instead.
